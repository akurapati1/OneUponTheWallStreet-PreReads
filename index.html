CHAPTER03

Three ideas Lynch never stops to explain.

III · 01

A share is a slice of a real business.

Not a number on a screen. Not a betting chip. When you buy stock in Apple, you own — proportionally — the factories, the cash, the brand, the lawsuits. The price moves because thousands of people are arguing every second about what that slice is worth.

Lynch's whole method depends on you remembering this. He buys companies, not tickers.

III · 02

Earnings drive everything, eventually.

In the short run, a stock can do anything — fashion, panic, hype, a tweet. In the long run, the only thing that pulls the price is whether the company is making more money than it used to.

That's why Lynch obsesses over earnings growth. A company doubling its profits will, over years, drag its share price up behind it. A company with shrinking profits will, over years, drag its share price down. Patience is the trick.

III · 03

The market is not one thing.

"The stock market went up today" is meaningless on its own. There are tiny growth companies, sleepy giants, cyclicals tied to the economy, turnarounds, and outright disasters — and they all behave differently. Lynch sorts every company he sees into six categories.

You don't need to memorize them yet. Just know: when Lynch says slow grower or stalwart or cyclical, he means specific, distinct things. The book will teach you those distinctions.
